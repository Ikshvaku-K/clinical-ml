# Model card: 30-day readmission risk (Diabetes-130, OMOP)

The structure follows Mitchell et al., *Model Cards for Model Reporting* (FAT* 2019).
The numbers in the table are generated by `make report` from `reports/*.json`. The
prose around them is written by hand and contains no numbers.

## Model details

- **What it is:** a gradient-boosted tree classifier (scikit-learn
  `HistGradientBoostingClassifier`, native categorical splits). It is followed by Platt
  recalibration fitted on a separate calibration split.
- **What it predicts:** the probability that an inpatient encounter of an adult with
  diabetes is followed by a readmission within 30 days.
- **Where it comes from:** milestones M1 (training), M2 (recalibration, threshold,
  uncertainty) and M3 (explanations, subgroup audit) of this repository. Each artifact is
  logged in MLflow; the run IDs are in the table.
- **Inputs:** 37 features built from OMOP CDM tables. They cover encounter
  administration, prior-year utilisation, diagnoses, diabetes medications and lab
  categories.
  - Age band and sex are features.
  - Race, ethnicity and payer are **not** features; they are used only for the audit.
- **License:** the code is in this repository. The training data is the UCI
  Diabetes 130-US Hospitals dataset (CC BY 4.0).

## Intended use

- **Intended:** methods research and demonstration. It shows how a readmission model is
  built on a common data model, recalibrated, given an operating point tied to
  follow-up capacity, explained, and audited for subgroup performance.
- **Intended users:** researchers and reviewers.
- **Out of scope:** clinical decision-making about real patients, or deployment
  anywhere. This holds even with local validation, because the data are from 1999–2008
  and the cohort and coding differ from current practice.

## Factors

- **Audited groups:**
  - race;
  - ethnicity, which the source records only as a Hispanic category inside race, so it
    repeats that group;
  - sex;
  - age, in four bands;
  - payer code: the three largest named codes, all other codes pooled, and missing.
- **Small groups:** a group needs a minimum number of test encounters and readmissions
  to be scored; smaller groups are listed but not scored.
- **Not audited:** intersections such as age × race, and site. The source does not
  identify hospitals in a usable way.

## Metrics

- **Discrimination:** AUROC and AUPRC.
- **Calibration:** slope, observed/expected and expected calibration error.
- **Clinical utility:** alert rate, sensitivity, PPV and NPV at the operating threshold,
  plus net benefit across thresholds.
- **Uncertainty:** class-conditional conformal prediction sets.
- **Subgroups:** the same metrics per group, each group's difference from the largest
  group in its attribute, and the largest between-group gap.
- **Confidence intervals:** all CIs come from a patient-level bootstrap, because patients
  contribute several encounters.

## Evaluation and training data

- **Split:** patient-level 60/20/20 into train, calibration and test. No patient
  appears in two splits.
- **Train split:** model fitting and grouped cross-validation.
- **Calibration split:** choosing the recalibration method and fitting it, the
  operating threshold, and the conformal quantiles.
- **Test split:** reporting only.

## Ethical considerations

- **Proxies.**
  - Excluding race from the features does not make the model race-blind. Payer,
    admission source and utilisation history can all correlate with race and
    socioeconomic status.
  - That is why the audit measures outcomes by group instead of relying on the
    exclusion.
- **Label bias.**
  - A readmission is only observed when it happens in a hospital in the dataset.
  - If some groups are more often readmitted elsewhere, their labels are incomplete,
    and the audit cannot detect that.
- **Care-process features.**
  - The strongest single push in some explanations comes from discharge dispositions
    that describe the care pathway, such as transfer within the same institution or to
    another short-term hospital.
  - They predict a coded readmission, but they may encode how care is organised rather
    than what the patient needs. A clinical reviewer should decide whether they belong
    in a deployed model.
- **Older patients.** Discrimination is lower and false positives are higher for the
  oldest patients. A follow-up programme driven by this model would spend relatively
  more of its capacity on older patients who would not have been readmitted.

## Caveats and recommendations

- **Local work before any use:** revalidate, recalibrate and re-derive the threshold
  on local data.
- **Small groups:** for several groups the CIs are too wide to say the model performs
  the same. Treat that as "unknown", not "fair".
- **Explanations:** SHAP values describe the model, not causes. A feature that raises
  predicted risk is not necessarily a lever for reducing actual risk.
- **Vocabulary:** standard OMOP concept mapping needs the OHDSI Athena vocabulary, which
  is not yet loaded. Source codes are used directly.
